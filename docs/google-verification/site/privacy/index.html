BooxDay Privacy Policy

Last updated: 26 October 2026

In short:

  • BooxDay has no server and no user accounts of its own. The developer never receives your data.
  • Your planner, including calendar data from Google or Outlook, is stored on your tablet.
  • Data leaves your tablet only for features you use: to Google or Microsoft to sync your calendar or save backups, and to OpenRouter when you use AI features with your own OpenRouter account.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking. Your data is never sold.

Who we are

BooxDay is a free, open-source planner app for Onyx BOOX tablets, made by an independent developer (GitHub: fortolano). Its full source code is public at github.com/fortolano/booxday, so anyone can check what it does. "We" on this page means the BooxDay developer.

What BooxDay keeps on your tablet

BooxDay stores your memos, tasks, handwriting, events, settings, and the calendar data it syncs, in its own private storage on the tablet. Other apps can't read this storage, with the exception described under "Other apps on your tablet". Uninstalling BooxDay deletes it.

Google account data

Connecting a Google account is optional. If you connect one, BooxDay asks for these permissions and uses them only as described here.

What BooxDay accesses

  • Google Calendar: the list of your calendars (names and colours) and the events on the calendars you choose to sync: title, start and end time, all-day flag, location, description and attendee email addresses.
  • Google Drive, only files BooxDay creates: the backup files BooxDay saves in a folder named "BooxDay Backups". BooxDay can't see or open any other file in your Drive.
  • Your Google account's email address and name, to show which account is connected.

How BooxDay uses it

  • To show your calendars and events on the tablet and keep them up to date.
  • To save changes you make in BooxDay to your Google Calendar: adding, editing or deleting an event. BooxDay writes to your calendar only when you save or delete an event. If you add attendees to an event, Google sends them invitations as it normally does.
  • To save backups of your planner to your Drive and restore them, only if you turn on Cloud Backup or tap Backup Now. Automatic backups (weekly by default) run only while Cloud Backup is on. A backup contains your planner data, including events you created in BooxDay and the email address of the account they belong to.
  • For the event reminders BooxDay shows on the tablet. Reminders are scheduled on the tablet; nothing is sent anywhere for them.
  • For the optional features you turn on that use your events: the sleep screen agenda, daily notes files and AI features, each described below.

Where it is stored

On your tablet, in BooxDay's private storage. BooxDay doesn't store your Google password; Google sign-in is handled by Google Play services on the tablet, which keeps the sign-in tokens.

Who it is shared with

BooxDay doesn't sell Google user data, doesn't use it for advertising, and doesn't send it to the developer. No person reads it. BooxDay doesn't use Google user data to develop, improve or train AI or machine-learning models. Google user data leaves your tablet only in these cases:

  • Google, to sync your calendar and Drive backups.
  • OpenRouter, when you use an AI feature that needs your schedule, on your own OpenRouter account (see "AI features").
  • Files you turn on: the sleep screen image and daily notes files, saved on your tablet (see below).
  • A crash report you choose to send (see "Crash reports").
  • Android's own device backup. If backup is turned on in your tablet's settings, Android may include BooxDay's data, which contains your synced calendar data, in the backup it saves to your Google account. This is an Android feature that you control in the tablet's settings.

BooxDay's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Microsoft Outlook data

If you connect an Outlook or Microsoft 365 account, BooxDay uses Microsoft sign-in with the permissions to read your basic profile and to read and write your calendars. It uses your calendars and events the same way as Google Calendar data above: stored on your tablet, synced with Microsoft, and sent to OpenRouter only by the AI features described below. Microsoft sign-in tokens are kept on the tablet by Microsoft's sign-in library.

AI features (OpenRouter)

AI features are optional and work only after you connect your own OpenRouter account. BooxDay has no AI key of its own. When you use an AI feature, BooxDay sends what that feature needs to OpenRouter, which passes it to the AI model provider that answers. OpenRouter and that provider handle it under their own privacy policies (OpenRouter's privacy policy). You can control which providers your account may use in your OpenRouter settings. With AI Features off (Settings → AI Settings), BooxDay sends none of your data to OpenRouter.

  • Optimize My Day, Daily Summary, Weekly Summary and Smart Suggestions send event details with your tasks and notes: titles, times, locations and, for some of them, attendee email addresses and part of the description. Optimize My Day includes events from all synced calendars, including calendars you have hidden from the calendar view, so it can avoid planning over them. These are sent only when you start the feature. The first time you use one, BooxDay tells you this and asks before sending anything. Smart Suggestions and Optimize My Day also send your AI options (how many suggestions you want, whether Vacation mode is on, and whether you want health or social ideas).
  • Reading your handwritten pages for search sends images of your BOOX Notes and memo pages in the background (task pages are read on the tablet), only while AI Features (Settings → AI Settings) and Enable Indexing (Settings → Boox Notes, off until you turn it on) are on, and an OpenRouter key is saved. These requests ask OpenRouter to use only providers that don't keep the data. They contain no calendar data.
  • Handwriting to text, summaries, translation and turning memos into tasks send the memo or task text or page images you choose.
  • Voice input: depending on the voice setting, your recording is sent to OpenRouter to be turned into text, or handled by your tablet's own speech recognition.

Keeping your calendar private with AI features. Free models may keep what they are sent. You choose: with credit on your OpenRouter account, BooxDay uses paid models, and you can pick the model for each job in AI Settings → AI Models. Turning on Zero Data Retention in your OpenRouter account's privacy settings makes OpenRouter use only providers that keep no copy. BooxDay doesn't change these choices for you.

Your OpenRouter key is stored encrypted on the tablet and is never included in backups.

Files BooxDay saves outside its own storage

Only if you turn these on:

  • Today on sleep screen saves a picture of today's events (titles and times), tasks and memo in Pictures/BooxDay and hands it to the BOOX system, which copies it to show while the tablet sleeps. Turning the setting off deletes these pictures.
  • Daily notes saves a Markdown file per day (event times, titles and locations, tasks and memo text) in Documents/BooxDay/Daily Notes or a folder you pick.
  • Exports you make are saved where you choose.

Other apps that can read these folders, and any sync service you use for them, can see these files.

Other apps on your tablet

BooxDay offers a tasks feed that other apps on the tablet can read: your task text, due dates, priorities and whether tasks are done. It doesn't include calendar events, account details or handwriting.

Crash reports

If BooxDay closes unexpectedly, it saves a report on the tablet and offers it at the next start. Nothing is sent unless you tap Copy Report or Report on GitHub. The report contains technical details and recent lines from the app's own log. Email addresses in it are partly hidden, but it can contain some calendar event titles. Read it before posting it: reports on GitHub are public.

Other internet connections

  • Update check: shortly after it starts, BooxDay asks GitHub whether a newer version exists. The request carries only the app version.
  • Handwriting recognition for tasks runs on the tablet. Its language models are downloaded from Google the first time.

Like any internet request, these connections reveal your IP address to the server you connect to (Google, Microsoft, OpenRouter, GitHub).

Keeping and deleting your data

  • Your data stays on the tablet until you delete it or uninstall BooxDay.
  • Removing a Google or Outlook account in BooxDay (Settings → Calendar Accounts) deletes the events BooxDay downloaded for that account, and the account's email address, name and calendar choices. Removing the last Google account also withdraws BooxDay's access to your Google account.
  • You can withdraw BooxDay's access to your Google account at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Backups in your Google Drive stay in the "BooxDay Backups" folder until you delete them there.
  • Uninstalling BooxDay deletes everything in its private storage. Files it saved in shared folders (sleep screen pictures, daily notes, exports) stay until you delete them.

Security

Connections to Google, Microsoft, OpenRouter and GitHub use HTTPS. Sign-in tokens are kept by Google Play services and Microsoft's sign-in library, and BooxDay stores your keys in encrypted storage on the tablet.

Children

BooxDay is not directed at children under 13.

Changes to this policy

If BooxDay starts using your data in a new way, this page is updated before that version is released, with a new date at the top. Earlier versions are in the site's history on GitHub.

Contact
